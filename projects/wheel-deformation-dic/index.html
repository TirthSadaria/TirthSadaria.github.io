Non-Pneumatic Wheel: FEA, Video DIC and Test Rig

Modeled, simulated and measured how a non-pneumatic wheel deforms: CAD and ANSYS stiffness studies, a camera-only DIC method with 140 tracked markers, and a physical rig with a piezo element.

Feb – May 2026 · Smart Materials & Structures Research Center, University of Maryland · Advisor: Prof. Amr Baz

  • 140Markers tracked
  • 0.033 pxReprojection error
  • 16.35 mmPeak deformation
  • 225.56 N/mPart 1 stiffness

Problem

Spoke wheels deform in ways strain gauges capture poorly. The goal was a low-cost, full-field measurement that can be checked against simulation, on the lab's real wheel.

Approach

  1. Ran automated FEA stiffness studies of two wheel parts in ANSYS, then total-deformation simulations with a report per part.
  2. Calibrated a camera with a 15 mm checkerboard (60 frames sampled from a 24 fps video): reprojection error 0.0328 px.
  3. Tracked markers in OpenCV at 0.8597 mm per pixel and built DIC-style displacement and strain maps from a Delaunay mesh.
  4. Modeled the full non-pneumatic wheel in CAD, matching blade curvature and overall dimensions of the lab wheel, and simulated it in ANSYS at 20 mm imposed displacement.
  5. Set up a physical test rig with camera and wheel, and logged piezo-element voltage over a 5-minute run.

Results

  • FEA stiffness: Part 1 at 225.56 N/m (20.08 mm, 4.529 N) and Part 2 at 91.82 N/m (12.00 mm, 1.102 N).
  • DIC: 140 markers tracked across 514 frames, with peak deformation at frame 107 and a maximum relative deformation of 16.35 mm.
  • Deformation and equivalent-stress results for the full wheel, plus animated DIC heatmaps and side-by-side comparison videos.
